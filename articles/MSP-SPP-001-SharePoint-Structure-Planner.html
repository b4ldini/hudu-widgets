Step 01 of 05
Map Your Structure
1

Open the planner below and read the short “How this works” section at the top of it — it explains the five words you’ll see (site, library, folder, group, owners) with a filing-cabinet picture that makes them obvious.

2

Click + Add a site for each broad area of your organisation — think one filing cabinet per area: Finance, HR, Operations, Management…

3

Inside each site, add libraries — the drawers of that cabinet. A library is the level where “who can open it” gets decided, so give things their own library when a different circle of people should see them.

4

Inside libraries, add folders for everyday filing, just like the folders you have today. Click any name to rename it; hover over a row for its actions.

ℹ
Keep the filing shallow
Folders nested more than three levels deep get flagged by the health check — deep burrows are hard to find things in, and very long paths cause technical problems later. If you’re going deep, it usually means something deserves its own library.

Step 03 of 05
Decide Who Sees What
1

In Access groups, build your groups — a group is just a named list of people, like “the finance team”. Click names to put people in. Access is always given to groups rather than one person at a time, so when someone joins or leaves, it’s a single change.

2

Open details on each site and click in its owners — the people in charge of that area, who decide who else gets in. Every site needs at least two, so there’s always someone available.

3

Still in the site’s details, tick which groups use the site. Libraries are set to “same as site” unless you say otherwise — right for most everyday filing.

4

For anything a smaller circle should see (salaries, contracts, board papers), mark that library as restricted and tick just the group(s) who should have it.

ℹ
Ignore the “spo-…” labels
Each group carries a technical name like spo-finance-team. That’s for us, when we build your system — the planner suggests them automatically and they’re fine as they come. Just make sure the right people are in each group.

Step 04 of 05
Let the Health Check Tidy Your Plan
1

Keep an eye on the Health check panel on the right as you work. Every warning tells you what’s wrong and how to fix it — missing owners, empty groups, folders buried too deep.

2

If you mark a folder as “only some people should see this”, the health check will suggest Make it a library — one click turns it into its own restricted section with its own group. That’s deliberate: in SharePoint, privacy works properly at the library level, not on individual folders.

3

Aim for a panel of green ticks. A clean health check means your design follows best practice and we can build it exactly as drawn.

Worth Knowing
Why the Planner Has Opinions
Guardrails, Not Hurdles

The rules exist so your new system stays tidy for years

You’ll notice the planner nudges you in certain directions. Those nudges follow published SharePoint best practice — they’re the difference between a system that still makes sense in five years and one that slowly becomes a tangle nobody dares touch.

What It Steers You Towards
Habits that keep things simple
  • Privacy decided on whole libraries (drawers), so it’s always clear who can see what
  • Two owners for every site — someone’s always available when access decisions are needed
  • Access given to groups, not individuals — a joiner or leaver is one change, not fifty
  • Shallow filing — things stay findable, and links don’t break
What It Saves You From
The classic mistakes we see
  • Permissions sprinkled on individual folders — they silently break and nobody can say who sees what
  • One person holding all the keys — then going on holiday
  • Access granted person-by-person until every change needs an archaeology dig
  • Folders ten levels deep that nobody can ever find again
🔒
Nothing is uploaded anywhere

The planner runs entirely in your browser — there’s no account, no login, and your draft never leaves your computer while you work. The only copy we ever see is the one you choose to email us.

If Something’s Not Right
Common Questions

Questions & Answers

Click any question to expand the answer

Can I do this over several days?
Yes — your draft saves automatically and will be waiting when you come back, as long as you use the same browser on the same computer. If you want to be extra safe (or might switch machines), click Copy share code before you stop and keep the code somewhere — pasting it into Load a plan brings your design back anywhere.
My draft has disappeared
The draft lives in the browser you used — a different browser (say Edge instead of Chrome), a different computer, or clearing your browsing history each shows a blank planner. If you saved a share code or sent us an earlier version, paste it into Load a plan and it comes straight back. If not, we’re sorry — it will need redrawing, so do grab a share code as you go this time.
Can a colleague work on it too?
Yes, one at a time: click Copy share code and send them the code. They paste it into Load a plan on their own computer, make their changes, and send a new code back to you (or straight on to us). The latest code always carries the complete design.
One folder should only be visible to a few people
Tick “only some people should see this” on that folder. The health check will then offer Make it a library — one click turns it into its own restricted section and you just tick who should see it. This is on purpose: in SharePoint, privacy set on individual folders is fragile, so the planner makes the private thing its own drawer instead.
“Download plan file” doesn’t seem to do anything
Some views of the planner block file downloads. Use Copy as text instead — it carries exactly the same information — or open the planner in its own tab at this full-screen link, where downloads work normally.
I’ve made a mess and want to start again
Click Start over in the “Finished? Send it back” panel — it asks before wiping the draft. If there’s any chance you’ll want the current attempt back, click Copy share code first and keep the code.
I’m not sure how to represent something in my plan
Don’t guess — ask us. Email or call (details below), or send us your draft’s share code and we’ll look at it together and suggest how to lay it out. Folder notes are also handy: every folder has a note field where you can write things like “move the contents of the old Projects drive here”.

Partou VoIP / Connectivity Escalation Process

This document defines the process for reporting internet and VoIP connectivity issues at Partou sites to the Active8 service desk. It covers what information to gather before raising a fault, what to expect from us during an active outage, and how the escalation process works from investigation through to resolution.

Process Guide Audience: Partou IT & Active8 Engineers Last reviewed: May 2026
What to include when raising a fault
Have this information ready before contacting the service desk

The more complete your fault report, the faster we can act — particularly when engaging an ISP or VoIP provider, or arranging an engineer visit. As a minimum, please provide the following:

  • Site name and full address Including postcode — required for ISP fault logging and engineer dispatch
  • Nature of the fault Full internet outage, intermittent drops, slow speeds, VoIP only, Wi-Fi only — be specific
  • Time the fault was first noticed And any preceding events — e.g. power cut, router reboot, area maintenance works
  • Number of users / devices affected Whether this is a full-site outage or partial (e.g. one floor, one VLAN, one phone)
  • Phone / VoIP status Are desk phones showing "No Service" or "Unregistered"? Are calls dropping mid-call? One-way audio?
  • Triage steps already completed e.g. router rebooted, phones rebooted, cables checked, cabinet power cycled
  • Call forwarding number (if phones are affected) A mobile or alternative number to redirect inbound calls to while the fault is active
  • On-site contact name and direct number Required if an engineer visit may be needed — this person must be able to provide site access

Incomplete information won't prevent us opening an investigation, but it may delay ISP/VoIP provider engagement or engineer dispatch. We will request anything missing before proceeding to those steps.

What happens next
What to expect from Active8 once a fault is raised
  1. 1
    Ticket opened and provider engaged We raise a fault with your ISP and/or VoIP provider and begin chasing for a root cause and next steps. You will receive ticket updates every 30–60 minutes until the provider has confirmed the cause and their plan of action.
  2. 2
    VoIP call forwarding activated (if phones are down) If your phone system is affected, we will work with you to arrange call forwarding to a mobile or alternative number as soon as possible so the site remains contactable. Please have a forwarding number ready when you raise the fault.
  3. 3
    Site visit (if required) If the ISP reports no fault on the line, or if internal investigation is needed, we will attend site — bringing a replacement router from your backup stock where applicable. Stock availability is visible live in the asset register.
  4. 4
    Interim connectivity bridge Where an ISP fault is confirmed with a significant lead time for resolution, we will deploy a mobile hotspot to restore interim connectivity. This is a temporary measure — performance will differ from your main connection.
  5. 5
    Priority escalation If site attendance confirms no internal fault and the provider fault remains unresolved, we escalate to priority/critical status with a named contact and a written ETA.

Complete all steps below before escalating
Only raise with the Active8 service desk once all applicable checks are done and documented

Do not escalate until all triage steps are complete. Active8 only handles confirmed outages where on-site checks have already been performed. Incomplete escalations will be returned for triage before investigation begins.

DrayTek Vigor 2865ax — Router / WAN Gateway Check this first — all other devices depend on the router for connectivity
3 checks
  • 1
    Check the ACT LED — is the router powered on and running?

    The ACT (Activity) LED should be blinking green during normal operation.

    ACT LEDMeaningAction
    Blinking greenPowered on, normal operationFine — continue checks
    OffRouter is powered off or not runningCheck power — proceed to cabinet reboot
  • 2
    Check the DSL and WAN LEDs — is there a line signal and internet connection?

    These are the two most important LEDs for diagnosing a connectivity outage on the 2865ax.

    LEDStateMeaningAction
    DSLSolid greenDSL line synchronisedLine is up — check WAN
    DSLSlow blinkDetecting DSL lineWait — starting up
    DSLFast blinkDSL establishingWait — in progress
    DSLOffNo DSL signal detectedCheck cable — likely ISP line fault
    WANSolid / blinkingInternet connection activeWAN up — check routing/DNS
    WANOffNo internet connectionCheck cable and ISP status
    Record the exact state of both LEDs before escalating — this is the first thing we will ask for.
  • 3
    Check all physical cable connections

    Confirm the DSL or ethernet WAN cable from the master socket or ONT is fully seated in the correct port on the DrayTek. Check the LAN cable to the switch is secure at both ends. Look for any visibly damaged or dislodged cables.

    On an Openreach master socket, remove the lower half of the faceplate to access the test socket. Plugging directly into the test socket rules out internal wiring faults.
Unifi Switches Check after confirming the router is functioning
4 checks
  • 1
    Check power and status LED on each switch

    Each Unifi switch has a status LED on the front. It should be solid white when operating normally.

    LED stateMeaningAction
    Solid whiteNormal operationFine — continue
    Flashing blueAdoption pending (no controller)Note — may indicate controller issue
    Flashing amberFirmware upgrading or faultWait 5 min — do not power off
    OffNo powerCheck PDU — proceed to cabinet reboot
  • 2
    Check the uplink port from switch to router

    Identify which port connects the switch to the DrayTek. The port LED should be solid (green or white depending on model). An unlit uplink port means no physical link — re-seat the cable at both ends.

  • 3
    Check AP ports for active connections

    The AP LEDs on Unifi access points are disabled at Partou sites, so the switch port LED is the only visual indicator. Each active AP port should show a solid LED. An unlit port on a known AP connection means that AP has no link — check the cable at both the switch port and the wall or ceiling end.

  • 4
    Check the Unifi Network Controller and capture screenshots for the escalation ticket

    Open the Unifi Network app and navigate to the site. Capture two screenshots before escalating:

    Screenshot 1 — Device list: Go to the Devices view. A green dot means connected; grey means disconnected. Screenshot the full list.

    Screenshot 2 — Insights tab: Click on the switch to open its detail panel. Select the Insights / Statistics tab (bar chart icon). Screenshot the full panel including the usage graph.

    Both screenshots must be attached to the escalation ticket. If the controller is cloud-hosted and unavailable due to the outage, note this and rely on physical LED states instead.
Cabinet / PDU — Full Power Cycle Only perform this after completing visual LED checks and noting all states
4 steps
  • 1
    Switch off power to the PDU / cabinet

    All network equipment is on a single PDU in the cabinet. Switch it off at the PDU switch or at the wall socket feeding it. This will power down the DrayTek, all Unifi switches, and all APs simultaneously.

  • 2
    Confirm all lights have gone off

    Visually check the cabinet — every LED on the router, switches, and APs should be off. If any device is still lit, it may be on a separate power feed. Do not start the 60-second wait until all lights are confirmed off.

  • 3
    Wait a full 60 seconds from the point all lights went off

    This allows all capacitors to discharge and devices to fully reset. Do not shorten this — 60 seconds from confirmed power-off, not from when you flipped the switch.

  • 4
    Restore power and allow 5 minutes for everything to come back up

    Turn the PDU back on. The DrayTek will take the longest to boot — allow up to 5 minutes for DSL sync, WAN negotiation, and switches/APs to reconnect before testing connectivity. Re-check the DrayTek ACT, DSL, and WAN LEDs after this period.

    If connectivity is restored after the reboot — no escalation needed. If the DSL or WAN LED is still off, record the LED states and escalate.
Phones / VoIP Check registration status — helps confirm whether this is a WAN or QoS/routing fault
3 checks
  • 1
    Check phone registration status

    VoIP phones show "No Service", "Unregistered", or a similar error on-screen when they cannot reach the hosted PBX. This typically confirms the WAN is down or the SIP trunk is unreachable. If phones are working but computers have no internet, the issue may be routing or VLAN-specific.

    VoIP-only fault? If internet connectivity is fine but phones are still unregistered after a router reboot, the issue is likely with the SIP trunk, hosted PBX, or a firewall rule change. Note this clearly in the escalation ticket — it changes the provider we engage with.

  • 2
    Reboot the affected phones

    Power-cycle any unregistered desk phones by unplugging them from PoE for 30 seconds, then reconnecting. After 60 seconds, check whether they register. If some phones register and others do not, note the specific extension numbers or device locations of those still unregistered.

  • 3
    Set up call forwarding if phones remain down

    If the phone system cannot be restored quickly, arrange call forwarding to a mobile number before escalating. Include this forwarding number in the escalation ticket — it is required to ensure the site remains contactable during the outage.

Handling guidelines
Comms, VoIP-specific handling, ISP escalation, and backup router procedures
Client communication
  • Update the client ticket every 30–60 minutes until the provider confirms the root cause and next steps — not just an ETA.
  • Once a provider engineer is arranged to attend and investigate the fault, downgrade the ticket from P1. At this point the fault is outside our direct control and the priority classification should reflect that.
  • If dispatching a site visit, confirm the estimated arrival time with the on-site contact before attending.
  • Where a mobile hotspot bridge is deployed, document this clearly as a temporary measure and set expectations on performance differences versus the main connection.
  • If the phone system is also affected, confirm the call forwarding number is active before closing the initial call with the client.
VoIP-specific checks
  • If internet is up but VoIP phones remain unregistered, check SIP ALG on the DrayTek — it must be disabled for hosted VoIP to function correctly. Go to Firewall → General Setup and confirm SIP ALG is off.
  • Check QoS rules on the DrayTek — if SIP/RTP traffic is not prioritised, it may be starved during high-bandwidth periods, causing drops and one-way audio without a full outage.
  • Where only some handsets are affected, check whether they share a common switch port, VLAN, or PoE budget limit — this narrows the fault to a network segment rather than the trunk.
  • If re-registering phones over the mobile hotspot during a line fault, confirm the hotspot NAT does not block SIP — use a direct SIM hotspot (not tethering through an existing device's firewall) where possible.
  • Log which extension numbers or physical handsets are affected, not just "some phones" — this detail is required by the VoIP provider for provisioning investigation.
ISP escalation
  • Always obtain a provider fault reference number at ticket creation and log it against the client ticket.
  • If the ISP reports no fault on the line but the outage persists, arrange site attendance before re-engaging — this prevents repeated "no fault found" closures.
  • When escalating to priority/critical, request a named escalation contact, an updated ETA, and confirmation in writing.
  • Note the specific ISP and circuit type in the ticket — response SLAs differ between BT Openreach, Virgin, ITS, and others.
Backup router deployment
  • Confirm stock availability in the asset register before committing to a deployment during a call with the client.
  • Log the serial number of any deployed backup router against both the client ticket and the asset register.
  • Ensure the original router is returned to base and assessed for fault following resolution — do not leave faulty hardware on site.

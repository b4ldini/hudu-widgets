Article Overview

Your organisation’s email is protected by Microsoft Defender for Office 365, managed by Active8. Suspicious messages are held in quarantine instead of being delivered, and Defender emails each user a digest of what was held for them. As the designated superuser, you can also see and manage quarantined mail for everyone in your organisation.

This guide covers the day-to-day tasks: reading the digest email, finding a “missing” message in quarantine, releasing legitimate mail, blocking nuisance senders, and reporting anything suspicious — all through the Defender portal at security.microsoft.com.

⚠
Some messages can never be released — that’s deliberate

Messages Defender classifies as malware or high-confidence phishing are locked to administrators only. No release button will appear for them — Microsoft enforces this and it cannot be overridden from your side. If you believe one of these is genuinely legitimate, don’t try to work around it: contact Active8 and we’ll review and release it if it’s safe.

Step 01 of 05
Sign In to the Defender Portal
1

Open security.microsoft.com/quarantine in your browser and sign in with your work account, approving the MFA prompt as usual.

2

You land directly on the Quarantine page — a list of held messages with sender, subject, recipient and the reason each was held (Spam, Phish, Malware, etc.).

3

If you ever arrive at the portal home page instead, navigate via the left menu: Email & collaboration → Review → Quarantine.

4

Seeing only your own messages? Your superuser role may not be active — contact Active8 and we’ll check the Quarantine Administrator assignment.

ℹ
The portal is safe to explore
Nothing on the Quarantine page sends or deletes anything until you explicitly choose an action, and previewing a quarantined message is completely safe — it’s shown as inert text and images, with links and attachments disarmed.
↓
2
Step 02 of 05
Read the Daily Digest Email
1

Each user receives a daily digest listing messages held for them since the last one — sender, subject, and the date each was held. No mail held means no digest that day.

2

Next to each message are action links — Review message, Release, and Block sender. These work directly from the email; there’s no need to open the portal for a simple release, and no approval step — released mail goes straight to the inbox.

3

Release delivers the message to the recipient’s inbox exactly as originally sent. Block sender adds the sender to that user’s personal blocked list.

4

Encourage staff to treat the digest as their first stop for “I’m missing an email” — most cases resolve there without involving you or Active8.

⚠
Only release what you recognise and were expecting

Quarantine exists because these messages looked dangerous or unwanted. A convincing invoice or delivery notification from an unknown sender is exactly what phishing looks like. If in doubt, don’t release — preview it in the portal first, or ask Active8 to check it.

↓
3
Step 03 of 05
Find a Message in Quarantine

The most common superuser task: someone says “I was expecting an email and it never arrived.” Here’s how to find it.

1

On the Quarantine page, click Filter and narrow the list — the most useful filters are Recipient address (the person missing the email) and the Time received range.

2

You can also use the search box to look for the sender’s address or part of the subject line if you know them.

3

Click the message to open the details pane. Preview message shows the content safely; the details also show why it was held (spam, phish, impersonation, malware) and whether anyone has already released it.

4

Not in quarantine at all? Then Defender didn’t hold it — the message may have gone to the user’s Junk Email folder (worth checking first), been rejected before delivery, or never been sent. Contact Active8 and we can run a full message trace.

ℹ
Quarantine keeps messages for 30 days
After 30 days, held messages are permanently deleted and cannot be recovered by anyone — including Microsoft. If a user mentions a missing email from “a few weeks ago”, check quarantine sooner rather than later.
↓
4
Step 04 of 05
Release Messages & Manage Senders
1

Select the message and click Release. As superuser you can release mail held for any user, and for messages with several recipients you can release to all of them or only some.

2

During release you may be offered “Report to Microsoft” options — reporting a wrongly-held message as a false positive helps tune the filtering for everyone. Use it when the message was clearly legitimate.

3

The released message lands in the recipient’s inbox, usually within a few minutes. The quarantine entry stays in the list but shows as Released — a message can only be released once.

4

To stop a nuisance sender, select one of their messages and choose Block sender. This blocks them for that recipient. For an organisation-wide block or allow — or a sender who is repeatedly wrongly quarantined — contact Active8: we maintain the tenant-wide allow/block list centrally so it’s done safely and expires reviewed entries properly.

Quarantine reason Who can release it
Spam / bulk mail ✓ User or superuser
Phishing (standard confidence) ✓ User or superuser  (when in doubt, leave it and ask — a released phish can’t be un-released)
High-confidence phishing ✗ Active8 / admin only
Malware & blocked attachment types ✗ Active8 / admin only
⚠
A release is final — there’s no “un-release”

Once released, the message is in the user’s inbox and any links or attachments are live. If you release something and then have second thoughts, tell the recipient not to open it and contact Active8 immediately.

↓
5
Step 05 of 05
Report Suspicious Email
1

Anything suspicious that does reach an inbox should be reported with the Report button, built into Outlook (desktop, web and mobile) — select the message, then choose Report → Report phishing (or Report junk for plain spam).

2

Reporting sends the message to Microsoft and to the monitored security mailbox, and Defender automatically opens an investigation — it checks whether the same message reached anyone else and flags what should be removed.

3

Make this the standard habit across your team: report, don’t forward. Forwarding a phish around the office (“is this real?”) multiplies the risk; reporting it quietly deals with every copy at once.

4

If someone has already clicked a link or entered their password, that’s an incident, not a report — call Active8 straight away so we can secure the account.

Worth Knowing
What Defender Is Doing Behind the Scenes
Managed Protection

Most of the work happens before you ever see it

Every inbound message is checked for spam, spoofing, impersonation of your colleagues and domains, malicious attachments and dangerous links — and protection doesn’t stop at delivery. Knowing the moving parts helps explain the odd thing users notice.

Handled Automatically
You’ll rarely notice any of this
  • Safe Attachments opens every attachment in a Microsoft sandbox before delivery — this is why an email with an attachment can arrive a minute or two after one without
  • Safe Links rewrites links so they’re re-checked at the moment of click — hovering a link may show a long safelinks.protection.outlook.com address; that’s normal
  • Zero-hour purge retro-removes messages from inboxes if Microsoft learns they were malicious after delivery — mail can legitimately “disappear”
  • Impersonation checks watch for lookalikes of your key people and domains
Where You Come In
The judgement calls Defender leaves to people
  • Releasing wrongly-held mail your team is genuinely expecting
  • Spotting the “missing email” pattern — digest first, then quarantine search
  • Reporting suspicious messages so investigations start early
  • Telling Active8 about repeat offenders — both nuisance senders and legitimate ones that keep getting caught
🛡
Why you can’t release everything

Malware and high-confidence phishing are the two categories attackers most rely on someone releasing in a hurry. Microsoft locks these to administrators by design, and Active8 reviews any request to release one. It’s a deliberate speed bump — the small delay is the protection working, not a fault.

If Something Goes Wrong
Troubleshooting

Common Questions & Fixes

Click any problem to expand the fix

A legitimate email keeps getting quarantined every time
Release it as normal and use the Report to Microsoft → not junk option during release. If the same sender is caught repeatedly, contact Active8 with the sender’s address — we’ll add a proper tenant-level allow entry. Please don’t ask the sender to keep resending; each copy will be caught the same way.
I released a message but it hasn’t appeared in the inbox
Allow up to 15 minutes, and have the user check their Junk Email folder — release puts the message back into normal delivery, and Outlook’s own junk filter can still act on it. The quarantine entry will show Released once processed. Still missing after that? Contact Active8 with the subject line and time.
A user says an email never arrived, and it’s not in quarantine
Check the user’s Junk Email folder and their blocked senders list in Outlook first. If it’s genuinely nowhere, contact Active8 with the sender address, expected date and recipient — we can run a message trace that shows exactly what happened to it, including whether it was rejected before delivery or never sent at all.
The digest email isn’t arriving
First: a digest is only sent when there is something to report — a quiet day means no digest, which is normal. If mail is visibly sitting in the user’s quarantine but no digest ever arrives, check the digest itself hasn’t been swept into Junk, then contact Active8 to verify the notification settings.
I only see a “Request release” button, not “Release”
Your organisation is set up for self-service release, so on ordinary spam and phishing everyone should see a full Release button. If you or a user only get “Request release” on those, something isn’t right — contact Active8 and we’ll check the quarantine policy and your Quarantine Administrator role. The exception is malware and high-confidence phishing: no one but an admin ever gets a release button on those, by design.
An email “disappeared” from an inbox after it had arrived
That’s almost certainly zero-hour auto purge: Microsoft reclassified the message as spam or phishing after delivery and moved it to Junk or quarantine retrospectively. Check quarantine for it. If it was genuinely legitimate, release it and report it as a false positive.
Links in emails look strange — they all start with safelinks.protection.outlook.com
This is Safe Links working as designed. The original destination is still there at the end of the address, and the link is checked for danger at the moment it’s clicked. Nothing is wrong and nothing needs changing — users can click as normal.
A user was restricted from sending email
Defender restricts a mailbox that suddenly sends far more mail than normal — the classic sign of a compromised account, though a genuine bulk send (e.g. a newsletter to hundreds of recipients) can also trigger it. Active8 is alerted automatically when this happens. Don’t ask the user to keep trying; contact us and we’ll investigate and lift the restriction if all is well.

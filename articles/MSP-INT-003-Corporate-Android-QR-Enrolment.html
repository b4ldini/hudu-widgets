Setting up your new company Android phone

This guide takes you from a phone in your hand to a fully working company phone with your email, Teams and apps already on it. You do not need to be technical — every step is a picture and a sentence.

There is one thing you must understand before you begin, and it is on the red panel directly below: this process completely erases the phone.

About 30–45 minutes Android 10 or newer Wi-Fi needed Erases the phone
⚠ Stop — read this before you touch anything
This wipes the phone completely.
Everything on it will be gone.

A company Android phone can only be set up on a phone that has been factory reset — returned to the state it was in when it came out of the box. There is no way to convert a phone that is already in use. Once the wipe starts it cannot be undone or stopped, and nothing can be recovered afterwards.

🔑 Do this before anything else

Is Microsoft Authenticator on the phone you are about to wipe?

If it is, speak to IT before you reset the phone and ask them for a Temporary Access Pass. Halfway through this setup you have to sign in with your work account and prove it is you — and the app you would normally use to do that will have been erased minutes earlier. Without something to replace it, the phone is stuck at the sign-in screen and cannot be finished.

✓ Yes — it’s on this phone
Stop. Contact IT first.
Ask for a Temporary Access Pass (TAP). It is a short code IT generates for you that you type in instead of approving a notification — it gets you through the sign-in on the new setup.
Ask for it when you are ready to use it, not days in advance. A TAP is deliberately short-lived and will expire.
If you would rather, IT can instead help you move Authenticator to another device before the wipe. Either answer works — you just need one of them in place first.
Not sure / No
Check, then carry on.
Look through the phone’s apps for Microsoft Authenticator (a blue padlock icon). Also check for any other code app — Google Authenticator, Duo, a banking app that generates codes.
If there is genuinely nothing, and you approve sign-ins by text message or from a different phone, tablet or laptop, you are fine — carry straight on to Step 1.
If you are unsure, ask IT anyway. A TAP you did not need costs nothing; discovering you needed one after the wipe costs you the afternoon.
Word for word, this is all you need to say
“I’m setting up my new company Android phone and my Authenticator is on the phone I have to wipe. Please can I have a Temporary Access Pass?”
❓ What it actually is
A one-off code, usually a string of digits, that stands in for your usual approval — nothing to install and nothing to set up.
⏱ How long it lasts
A limited window set by IT, often an hour or so. Request it when you are about to start, not the day before.
📱 Where you type it
On the new phone, at Step 6, on the Microsoft sign-in screen where it would normally ask you to approve.
↻ Afterwards
Install Microsoft Authenticator on the new phone and set it up again. The pass stops working once it expires.

Step One · Do this days before, not minutes before
Save anything you want to keep

Work through the list below on the phone before you wipe it. Anything not on this list, and not copied off the phone, is gone for good.

🔑
The one that catches people out: your Authenticator app
Worth repeating, because it is the step people skip: if Microsoft Authenticator (or any other code app) is on this phone, wiping it destroys your ability to sign in — including the sign-in you need halfway through this very setup. Speak to IT and get a Temporary Access Pass before you reset the phone. Do not start the wipe until that is sorted — see for exactly what to ask for.
📸Photos & videos
Nothing in your gallery survives a wipe.
How: plug the phone into a computer with its USB cable and copy the DCIM folder off — or open Google Photos and let it finish backing up before you start.
💬WhatsApp
Chats and photos are stored on the phone, not in the cloud, unless you back them up.
How: WhatsApp → Settings → Chats → Chat backup → Back up. Wait for it to say it has finished.
👤Contacts
Numbers saved to the phone itself vanish. Numbers already in a Google or work account are safe.
How: Contacts app → menu → Manage contacts → Export to a file, then email that file to yourself.
✉Text messages
SMS history is not backed up by anything as standard.
How: if you need them, install SMS Backup & Restore from the Play Store and save the backup file to your computer or email.
🏦Banking & other apps
Apps that "remember" you will not remember you afterwards.
How: make sure you know the usernames and passwords, and that any app that texts you a code can still reach you.
💳Google Pay / wallet cards
Cards, tickets and passes are removed with the wipe.
How: nothing to save — just be aware you will need to add cards again afterwards, and a company phone may not allow it.
☁Work email & files
Already safe. Anything in Outlook, Teams, OneDrive or SharePoint lives on Microsoft's servers, not the phone.
How: nothing to do. It all comes back on its own once you sign in.
🔒Passwords in your browser
Safe if they were syncing to a Google or Microsoft account.
How: open Chrome → Settings and check it shows an account name at the top. If it says "Sign in", they are only on this phone.
⚠
Remove your personal Google account before you reset
Android has a theft protection feature that can lock a phone after a reset until the previous Google account is entered. Avoid it entirely: go to Settings → Accounts, tap your Google account and choose Remove account before you do the reset in Step 3. Do this last, after all your backups have finished.
Final check — tick all five before you go any further
I have spoken to IT, and my sign-in is sorted — a Temporary Access Pass is issued, or my Authenticator has been moved to another device.
Photos and videos are copied off the phone, or fully backed up.

Step Four · The bit nobody guesses
Tap the screen six times to open the QR scanner
The hidden step

There is no button for this. You have to tap.

On the very first screen after the wipe, tapping the same blank spot six times opens a hidden QR code scanner. This is how Android is designed to work — the option is deliberately hidden so that ordinary users do not stumble into it. Nothing on screen tells you it is there.

Step Eight · Five minutes
Four quick checks, then you’re done

These four take a couple of minutes each and save a support call later.

Open Outlook and check your email arrives. Sign in with the same work account if it asks. Your calendar and contacts come with it.
Check your contacts show up when someone rings. Open the Contacts app — your work contacts should be listed. If a known number rings and shows no name, tell IT.
When you save a new contact, save it to your work account — not to “Device” or “Phone”. Android asks the first time and then remembers. Anything saved to “Device” is backed up by nothing and is lost if the phone is.
Take a screenshot and try to open it. If the phone says it needs an image viewer, the gallery app has not been switched on yet — tell IT and they will push it to you. It is a two-minute fix at their end.
📷
Photos you take on this phone are not backed up automatically
A company phone has no Google Photos or Google One backup. If you take pictures for work, put them somewhere safe the same day — upload them to OneDrive, or turn on camera backup inside the OneDrive app (open OneDrive → your account picture → Camera backup) and allow it access to your photos. Ask IT if you would like a hand with this at handover.
✓

That’s it — the phone is set up

Your email, calendar, Teams and work apps are all in place, the phone is protected, and IT can support it remotely. From here on it behaves like any other phone — the differences are listed just below so nothing takes you by surprise.

What is different about a company phone

None of this is a punishment or a comment on you — it is what keeps company information safe, and most of it you will simply never notice. Knowing it in advance saves a puzzled phone call in a fortnight.

Works as normal
  • Calls, texts, camera and web browsing
  • All your work email, files and Teams
  • Approved extra apps from the Play Store
  • Fingerprint or face unlock
  • Wi-Fi at home and at any site
Different from a personal phone
  • You cannot add a personal Google account
  • The Play Store only shows approved apps
  • You cannot factory reset it yourself
  • Updates install themselves, automatically
  • Connecting it to a computer charges only — no file transfer
  • IT can wipe it remotely if it is lost or stolen
👁
What IT can and cannot see
IT can see which apps are installed, the phone’s name and serial number, whether it is up to date, and whether it is locked with a PIN. IT cannot read your texts, listen to your calls, look through your photos, or see which websites you visit. This is a work phone, so treat it as one — but nobody is reading over your shoulder.

When something goes wrong

THE TWELVE THINGS THAT ACTUALLY HAPPEN

I tapped the screen loads of times and nothing happened

Three things to check, in this order:

  • Are you on the very first screen? The trick only works on the "Hi there" / "Welcome" screen straight after a wipe. Press Back until you get there.
  • Are you tapping blank space? Tapping the Start button, the language name or any other control does not count. Aim for the middle of the screen.
  • Keep going. Six is standard but some handsets want a few more — tap up to ten times, in the same place, at a steady pace.

Still nothing? Use the alternative route: go through setup normally, and when asked for a Google account type afw#setup as the email address.

It says “Can’t set up device” after I scanned the code

This almost never means the QR code is faulty, even though it reads that way. The usual causes are that the code has expired or been replaced, that your account is not yet cleared for a company phone, or that Wi-Fi dropped mid-way.

Try once more on a solid Wi-Fi connection. If it fails again, stop and contact IT — ask them to confirm the QR code is current and that your account is set up for a company-owned device. Repeated attempts will not fix it.

The camera will not read the QR code
  • Hold the phone 20–30 cm away, square on rather than at an angle.
  • Turn the brightness up on whatever is displaying the code.
  • If it is on a computer screen, zoom in so the code fills more of the screen — and zoom out again if that makes it worse. Both extremes cause failures.
  • Wipe the camera lens. On a phone fresh out of a box, peel the protective film off first.
  • A printed copy often scans more easily than a screen.
The phone restarted in the middle of setting up

This is the one genuinely serious mishap. A phone interrupted partway through can end up half-registered — it looks finished but is not actually protected or properly connected.

Do not try to carry on. Factory reset the phone and start again from Step 3. The second run takes far less time because there is nothing left to back up. Tell IT so they can clear away the half-finished record at their end.

I can’t approve the sign-in — my Authenticator was on this phone

This is the most common way people get stuck, and it is fixable but it needs IT. Contact them and ask for a Temporary Access Pass — a one-time code you type in instead of approving a notification. It is valid for a short period, so ask for it when you are ready to use it.

Once you are signed in, reinstall Authenticator and set it up again on this phone. Next time, move it before you wipe.

It is asking for a Google account and password I don’t recognise

You have hit Android's anti-theft protection. It triggers when a phone is reset while a Google account is still signed into it, and it demands that same account before the phone can be used.

Enter the Google account that was on the phone before the wipe, if you know it. If you do not, contact IT — on a company-supplied handset they may be able to help, but it can require proof of purchase from the supplier. This is exactly what removing the Google account in Step 1 avoids.

It has been sitting on the same screen for ages

Give it more time than feels reasonable. Downloading and applying the settings genuinely can take 15 minutes or more on a slow connection, and the progress indicator often does not move.

Plug the phone in and leave it. Only if nothing at all has changed after 30 minutes should you assume it is stuck — and then the fix is a factory reset and a fresh start, not a restart.

I took a screenshot and it says I need an image viewer

Nothing is broken. A company phone starts with most built-in apps switched off, and the gallery is sometimes one of them. It needs turning on centrally — you cannot fix it from the phone.

Tell IT, mentioning the make and model of your phone. They enable the right gallery app for that handset and it appears within a few minutes.

The Play Store won’t let me install an app I want

Expected. The Play Store on a company phone only offers apps that have been approved, and there is no way around it from the phone — that is the point of the control, not a fault.

If you need something for work, ask IT to add it. Personal apps generally will not be approved for a company handset.

Where is Company Portal? I used it on my personal phone

It is not used on company-owned phones. Company Portal is for adding a personal phone to work; here the whole phone is company-managed from the moment it is set up, so the equivalent app is Microsoft Intune. It installs itself, and in day-to-day use you will not need to open it.

Can I put my personal Google account on it?

No — personal Google accounts are blocked on this phone, so Gmail, personal Google Photos and personal Play Store purchases are not available on it.

That is a deliberate decision. It keeps the line between work and personal clean, and it means nothing personal of yours can be caught up if the phone ever has to be wiped.

My phone was lost or stolen — what happens now?

Tell IT immediately. They will wipe it remotely so nothing on it can be reached, whether or not the thief knows your PIN.

Nothing of yours is lost with it: your email, calendar, contacts, Teams messages and files all live on Microsoft's servers. Once you have a replacement, set it up with this same guide, sign in, and everything comes back. Note that you will need a way to approve that sign-in without the missing phone — ask IT for a Temporary Access Pass at the same time as you report it.

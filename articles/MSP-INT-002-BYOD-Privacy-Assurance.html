Your Privacy

If you use your own phone or tablet for work, you have every right to ask what we can see on it. This article answers that question in full — not in principle, but item by item.

The short version: nobody is reading your messages, looking at your photos, or checking where you are. What we are switching on exists to answer two questions and two questions only — is the device patched, and is it locked — and it has been built deliberately so that we can answer them without being able to see anything else about your phone.

Background
Why Any of This Exists

Cyber Essentials — the certification the Group is working towards — sets two requirements for any device that can reach company information.

1

Security updates must be installed within 14 days of the manufacturer releasing them.

2

The device must have a basic set of security settings in place: a passcode or PIN, encryption switched on, and no tampering with the phone’s built-in protections (jailbreaking or rooting).

We have to be able to demonstrate to an assessor that both of those are true. That is the entire purpose of what we are switching on at 12pm on Monday.

Two questions, and nothing more
Everything described in this article traces back to “is it patched?” and “is it locked?” If a piece of information does not help answer one of those two questions, it is not collected.

The Detail
Where the Line Sits
On Your Own Phone or Tablet

Exactly what is visible to us — and what never is

On a personal device, enrolment covers the work side only. On an iPhone or iPad it creates a separate, sealed space for work data; on Android it creates a separate work profile. In both cases the personal side of the phone is not inventoried, not readable, and not ours to control.

What We Can See
The evidence an assessor asks for
  • That you have signed into a work app with your work account, and which work apps the protection has applied to
  • A basic device record: device name, make, model, and the version of iOS or Android it is running
  • On Android, the security patch level, so we can confirm updates are actually arriving rather than just being pushed
  • Whether the phone has been jailbroken or rooted
  • Whether the work app PIN has been set
What We Cannot See
Your personal life stays personal
  • Your photos, messages, WhatsApp or call history
  • Your contacts — nothing about contacts is configured on a personal device
  • Which personal apps you have installed, or how you use them
  • Your location — at any time, ever
  • Your browsing history, personal email, or any personal account
  • Anything stored on your phone outside the work apps themselves
🔒
Why the device record exists at all

Every device that signs into a work account creates one of these records — there is no such thing as an invisible device. That record is what proves to an assessor that a supported, patched, locked device is accessing company data. It is the whole reason it exists.

On location specifically: it is not collected and not configured on any device we manage — personal or company-issued.

2
A Fair Question
Why the Work Apps Ask for Their Own PIN

A fair question, since almost every phone already has a passcode or Face ID.

The work app PIN is a second lock on the work apps specifically, rather than a check on your phone. It exists so we can evidence to an assessor that company data sitting on a personal device is behind a lock, without having to inspect your device to prove it. “Almost everyone has a passcode” is not something we can put in front of an assessor; “the work apps require a 6-digit PIN” is.

Face ID or Touch ID opens the apps day to day — the PIN is the fallback.

✓
The same logic applies to encryption
The policy encrypts the work data inside the apps — so we are protecting our data rather than inspecting your device.

The Most Common Question
About Contacts

Contacts come up more than anything else, so it is worth being exact about what is configured and what is not.

Device What happens with contacts
 Your own iPhone or iPad Nothing is synced. We do not push the Group’s contact directory to personal devices, and no contact sync is configured for them. Outlook has its own optional setting to save its contacts into your phone’s address book — our policy does not block it, but it is off unless you choose to switch it on. That is your decision, not ours. Your personal contacts are never read, never uploaded, and never visible to us.
🤖 Your own Android Work contacts can show in your dialler. The work profile is permitted to answer caller-ID lookups, so a work number rings with a name rather than just digits. That is a lookup, not a copy — the contact stays inside the work profile, and your personal contacts stay on the personal side.
📱 Company-issued handset Contact sync is configured and runs both ways. The Group’s contact directory syncs into the handset, and contacts saved on the handset sync back up into the work account — which is what makes them recoverable. So keep personal contacts off a company phone, or expect them to end up in the company directory.

The App You Will See
The Company Portal App

Company Portal is Microsoft’s app that sets your device up for work. It is used on every device with work access — your own iPhone, iPad or Android, and any company-issued handset. If you removed it while the Apple migration was running, please install it again and sign in.

1

Sets the device up in the first place. On a personal device it establishes the work side only — a sealed work space on iPhone and iPad, a separate work profile on Android. It does not take over your phone.

2

Puts the Group’s approved apps in one place, so you are not hunting through the App Store or Play Store.

3

Shows you your device’s status — which is the part worth understanding.

It tells you what is wrong, and how to fix it

If your device stops meeting one of the security requirements, you are not left guessing. You get an email, and Company Portal shows the device as Not compliant with the specific item listed and a “How to resolve” link next to it. Tap it and it walks you through the fix.

ℹ
It is not a monitoring app
Company Portal shows you your own device’s status. It does not give us anything beyond what is set out in this article. It is a window for you, not a camera for us.

Common Questions

Questions & Answers

Click any question to expand the answer

Can you read my messages or see my photos?
No. Your photos, messages, WhatsApp and call history are not visible to us on any device — personal or company-issued. There is no setting anywhere that would let us look, and nothing of the sort is configured.
Can you see where I am?
No — at any time, ever. Location is not collected and is not configured on any device we manage, personal or company-issued. This is worth stating twice because it is the question people most expect a caveat on. There isn’t one.
Can you wipe my personal phone?
No. On a personal device we can only remove the work side — the sealed work space on iPhone and iPad, or the work profile on Android. Your photos, apps, messages and everything else on the personal side are untouched, and there is no mechanism for us to wipe the whole device. On a company-issued handset, which belongs to the Group, a full wipe is possible.
Why do the work apps need their own PIN when my phone already has one?
Because we have to evidence the lock to an assessor without inspecting your phone. “Almost everyone has a passcode” is not evidence; “the work apps require a 6-digit PIN” is. Day to day you will use Face ID or Touch ID — the PIN is only the fallback.
Will my work contacts flood my personal address book?
Not on a personal device. On an iPhone or iPad nothing is synced — Outlook has its own optional setting to save its contacts into your address book, and it stays off unless you switch it on. On a personal Android, work contacts can be looked up so a work number rings with a name, but they are not copied to the personal side. On a company-issued handset contact sync does run both ways, so keep personal contacts off a company phone.
Can you see which apps I have installed?
Only work apps, and only which of them the protection has applied to. Your personal apps — and how you use them — are invisible to us. On a personal device the personal side is not inventoried at all.
What happens if my device is flagged as not compliant?
Nothing dramatic, and nothing sudden. You get an email listing exactly what needs fixing, and Company Portal shows the item with a “How to resolve” link that walks you through it. If it is left unresolved, access to work data on that device is withdrawn until it is sorted — but no device is wiped and no personal data is touched at any stage.
Will I have to keep changing my password?
No. Passwords are never forced to expire on any of these devices. Following NCSC guidance, forced rotation makes passwords worse rather than better, so we do not do it.
I deleted Company Portal — do I need it back?
Yes, please reinstall it and sign in. Company Portal is used on every device with work access. If you removed it while the Apple migration was running, that is understandable — but it is the app that sets up the work side and shows you your device’s status, so work access depends on it.
My phone is too old to update — what now?
Get in touch and we will look at the options with you. If a device no longer receives security updates, the work apps will stop opening on it, because we can no longer meet the 14-day patching rule and the certification allows no exception.
I would like to see these settings for myself
Ask — genuinely. We are happy to walk through any of the policies with you and show you what is configured. This only works if people are comfortable with it.
